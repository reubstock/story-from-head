The Summer I Dug to China

told by Sam Ortega · for Mabel, 6 · a memory from age 9
Sam, age 9

It started with a library fact: dig straight down, come out in China. I had a spoon and the whole summer.

Sam, age 9

By August the hole was up to my chest. The spoon became a trowel. The trowel became a borrowed shovel.

Sam, 9 — and Grandpa Tino

Grandpa found me down there. He didn't laugh. He climbed in, took the shovel, and dug for an hour beside me.

Sam, age 9

“I tried this once too,” he said. “Didn't reach China. Reached your grandmother's yard.” We filled it in by moonlight.

The Boy Who Tunneled Into the Blackout

a Story from Head crossover · Sam Ortega × Rosa Delgado · two friends, one world

When Sam's tunnel finally broke through, it didn't come up in China. It came up through the cold floorboards of a house where every light had died at once. A girl was waiting there with a single candle, unsurprised, as if she'd been told a boy would arrive from below.

“Is this China?” he asked. Rosa looked at the dark street full of neighbors holding matches — and decided the kindest answer was a tour. She never said no. She just lifted the candle and showed him a country that was really only her village, the night the power failed.
